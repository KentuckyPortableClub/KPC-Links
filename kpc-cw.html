QRQ Speed Ladder

QRQ is ham shorthand for "send faster." Start at an easy speed. Every one you get right speeds up, and every miss slows it back down. Three misses and the run is over. Your score is the fastest speed you got right.

Copy ladder: hearing it again is fine, but that one won't speed you up. The ladder plays a clean signal (no noise or fading) so everyone's scores compare fairly.

🎓 Learn CW: from zero to 30 WPM

One step at a time: meet a new character, copy it by ear, then send it with your key. Characters are always sent at full speed (20 WPM) so you learn the sound, with extra space between them at first. After all the letters, numbers and signs come words, call signs, POTA exchanges, then speed steps to 30 WPM. Tap any dot on the map to jump around. Your progress stays on this device.

⚔️ Expert Gauntlet

For seasoned ops. Five rounds, each harder: 1 fast copy (35 WPM and up) · 2 cut numbers and tricky calls · 3 pileup from hell · 4 rough band · 5 sloppy fists. No hints, one AGN per item, 30 seconds to answer. Three busts and you're out.

Live CW Spots: Click to Listen

POTA and SOTA stations spotted on CW in the last 30 minutes, newest at the top. Pick what to SHOW, pick the receiver to LISTEN ON, then tap 🎧 LISTEN on a spot and that WebSDR opens already tuned to it.

SHOW

AUTO picks the receiver at a good distance from each station (radio signals skip over nearby receivers: a Pennsylvania station on 20 m is often hard to hear in Pennsylvania). You can still pick a receiver yourself. Strong signals decode best. Weak or fading signals decode poorly or not at all, so if the text is garbled, try another receiver or a stronger station.

Where to Listen

  1. Open a WebSDR (a spot's 🎧 LISTEN button, or a band below). It opens in its own tab. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer; phones and Safari can't share tab audio, so on a phone use 🎤 USE MICROPHONE next to your radio instead.
  2. In the WebSDR tab, make sure CW mode is on and the volume slider is up. If the WebSDR has a narrow CW filter, use it. You should hear the tone clearly; if you can't hear it, the decoder can't either.
  3. Come back to this tab and tap 🖥 DECODE A TAB (WEBSDR).
  4. In the pop-up, choose Chrome Tab (Edge calls it Tab), click the WebSDR tab, turn on Also share tab audio, then click Share. Keep the WebSDR tab open; you can switch back here.
  5. The CW shows as a bright line in the waterfall. AUTO-FIND locks onto the strongest one; turn AUTO-FIND off and tap a line to stay on a different station.
  6. Strong, steady signals decode best. Weak, fading or crowded signals will come out garbled or blank. Try another receiver or a stronger station.
