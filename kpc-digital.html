The Major Digital Modes

Tap a goal to see the modes that fit. Every mode here works with a computer (or phone) and your radio's audio.

POTA FAVORITEHF • WEAK SIGNAL

FT8

The most popular mode on HF. Fully automated 15-second transmissions that get through when you can barely hear anything. Contacts are short: calls, grid, signal report.

BANDWIDTH
50 Hz
SOFTWARE
WSJT-X (free)
EASE
BEST FOR
Making contacts on low power, poor conditions, quick POTA logs
SEE FT8 FREQUENCIES →
POTAHF • WEAK SIGNAL

FT4

FT8's faster sibling: 7.5-second transmissions, built for contests. A little less sensitive, but twice the contact rate when a pileup builds.

BANDWIDTH
90 Hz
SOFTWARE
WSJT-X
EASE
BEST FOR
Contests and busy runs
SEE FT4 FREQUENCIES →
HF • KEYBOARDWEAK SIGNAL

JS8Call

FT8's weak-signal modulation turned into a real conversation mode. Type messages, relay through other stations, leave messages in inboxes. Popular with preparedness groups.

BANDWIDTH
50 Hz (normal speed)
SOFTWARE
JS8Call (free)
EASE
BEST FOR
Real QSOs on low power, store-and-forward messaging
SEE JS8 FREQUENCIES →
HF • KEYBOARDCLASSIC

PSK31

The original keyboard-to-keyboard sound card mode. Live typing at about the speed of a good typist, with a lot of narrow signals sharing one slice of the band.

BANDWIDTH
~31 Hz
SOFTWARE
fldigi (free)
EASE
BEST FOR
Real-time chats
SEE PSK31 FREQUENCIES →
HF • CONTESTSCLASSIC

RTTY

Radioteletype — the oldest digital mode, still huge in contests and DXpeditions. Wider and louder than the newer modes, and fun to work in a contest.

BANDWIDTH
~250 Hz
SOFTWARE
fldigi, MMTTY, N1MM+
EASE
BEST FOR
RTTY contests (like the ARRL RTTY Roundup), DX
SEE RTTY FREQUENCIES →
HF • KEYBOARDVERY ROBUST

Olivia

Slow but almost unbreakable keyboard mode — it copies through static, fading and interference that wipes out PSK. Used by some nets and preparedness groups.

BANDWIDTH
250–1000 Hz (common: 8/250, 16/500)
SOFTWARE
fldigi
EASE
BEST FOR
Nets and chats in rough conditions
GET FLDIGI →
HF + VHFEMAIL

Winlink

Real email over radio, with attachments and forms. Connect to a gateway station on HF (VARA HF or ARDOP) or VHF (VARA FM or packet). The backbone of a lot of ARES and emergency work.

MODEMS
VARA HF/FM, ARDOP, packet
SOFTWARE
Winlink Express (Windows), Pat (Linux/Mac/Pi)
EASE
BEST FOR
Emergency messaging, email from the field

VHFPOSITION & TEXT

APRS

Automatic Packet Reporting System — beacons your position, weather and short text messages to a nationwide network. Track your activation trip or a fox-hunt team on a map.

FREQUENCY
144.390 MHz (North America)
GEAR
APRS radio, or an HT + phone app (APRSdroid, aprs.fi viewers)
EASE
BEST FOR
Tracking, short messages, ISS digipeater
SEE STATIONS ON APRS.FI ↗
HF + VHFPICTURES

SSTV

Slow-scan TV — send and receive pictures, about a minute each. Fun from a park ("here's my view") and during the Space Station's SSTV events on 145.800 MHz.

BANDWIDTH
~2.5 kHz (voice-wide)
SOFTWARE
MMSSTV, QSSTV, Robot36 (phone)
EASE
BEST FOR
Picture swaps, ISS events
SEE SSTV FREQUENCIES →
HFDIGITAL VOICE

FreeDV

Open-source digital voice for HF SSB rigs — no hiss, no static, as long as the signal holds. A computer (or a small FreeDV box) does the codec.

FREQUENCY
14.236 MHz USB is the usual 20 m meeting spot
SOFTWARE
FreeDV (free)
EASE
BEST FOR
Experimenting with clean-sounding HF voice
FREEDV.ORG ↗
VHF/UHFDIGITAL VOICE

DMR

Digital Mobile Radio — cheap radios, lots of repeaters and worldwide "talkgroups" (Brandmeister). You need a free radio ID and a code-plug for your radio. A hotspot puts you on the network from home or camp.

NEEDS
DMR radio, free ID from RadioID.net, code-plug
EASE
BEST FOR
Repeater and worldwide talkgroup chats
GET A RADIO ID ↗
VHF/UHFDIGITAL VOICE

D-STAR & System Fusion

The two other big digital voice systems. D-STAR is Icom's; System Fusion (C4FM) is Yaesu's and often shares repeaters with regular FM. Both link repeaters over the internet (reflectors, WIRES-X).

NEEDS
A D-STAR (Icom) or Fusion (Yaesu) radio; D-STAR gateway access needs registration
EASE
BEST FOR
Local repeaters that run them; linked nets

PARKS ON THE AIR

Activating a Park on FT8

FT8 counts as a data contact for POTA. Low power, quiet campsites and automatic logging make it a great way to get your 10.

YOUCQ POTA KZ4CP EM78
HUNTERKZ4CP W8ABC EN81
YOUW8ABC KZ4CP -12
HUNTERKZ4CP W8ABC R-08
YOUW8ABC KZ4CP RR73
HUNTERKZ4CP W8ABC 73

Each line is one 15-second transmission. WSJT-X sends these for you once you double-click a caller.

  1. Spot yourself on pota.app with your park and frequency — hunters look there first.
  2. Call "CQ POTA" — WSJT-X lets you replace plain CQ with CQ POTA (type it into the CQ message).
  3. Pick a clear spot in the waterfall and keep transmitting there; turn on "Hold Tx Freq".
  4. Watch the clock — no internet in the park, so sync before you leave or use GPS time.
  5. Fix your log afterwards: add your park reference to every contact, then upload to POTA.
  6. Check propagation on PSKReporter — it shows where your CQs are being heard.
