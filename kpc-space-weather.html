HF OPERATOR VIEW

What Do These Numbers Mean For Radio?

There is no single number that tells you whether a band is open. KPC's field approach is to combine solar activity, geomagnetic activity, time of day, band, antenna, noise level and what you actually hear.

Start With Kp

0–2: generally quiet. 3–4: unsettled to active. 5+: geomagnetic storm territory. A higher Kp can make HF paths less stable, especially on higher-latitude paths.

Then Look At F10.7

F10.7 is a standard solar radio-flux indicator. Higher values generally mean more solar activity and can support higher HF bands when the ionosphere cooperates.

Watch Bz & Solar Wind

A sustained southward Bz (negative Bz) can allow more solar-wind energy to couple into Earth's magnetosphere. Faster solar wind can also accompany disturbed conditions. Neither value alone predicts your exact path.

How To Read Kp

Kp 0–2 • QuietOften a calmer starting point for HF, although band openings still depend on propagation and local conditions.
Kp 3–4 • Unsettled / ActivePaths may become more variable. Try more than one band and listen before deciding a band is dead.
Kp 5–9 • StormGeomagnetic disturbance is strong enough to cause significant HF changes. Polar and high-latitude paths can be particularly affected.

Portable tip: don't stop at one band.

If 20m is quiet, check 17m, 15m, 30m or 40m. Space weather changes the odds; it does not replace listening.

Portable tip: your antenna matters.

Height, efficiency, ground conditions, power, feed line and local noise can easily outweigh a modest change in the solar numbers.

NOAA SPACE-WEATHER SCALES

R, S & G — Know The Difference

NOAA uses three separate scales. They describe different effects, so an R2, S2 and G2 are not interchangeable.

R • Radio Blackout

R—

Driven by solar flares and focused on HF radio degradation on the sunlit side of Earth.

R1Minor
R2Moderate
R3Strong
R4Severe
R5Extreme

S • Solar Radiation

S—

Driven by energetic solar particles. Higher levels can affect HF communications on polar routes and affect satellites and navigation systems.

S1Minor
S2Moderate
S3Strong
S4Severe
S5Extreme

G • Geomagnetic Storm

G—

Driven by disturbances in Earth's magnetic environment. Higher levels can produce HF fading or outages, especially at higher latitudes.

SOLAR ACTIVITY EXPLAINED

The Other Numbers Worth Knowing

Sunspots

Sunspots mark active regions on the Sun. More solar activity can support higher HF bands, but sunspot number by itself does not tell you what will happen on your exact path.

X-Ray Flux

GOES X-ray class gives a quick look at solar flare activity. C-class is lower-level, M-class is stronger, and X-class is the most intense category. Strong flares can cause shortwave/HF radio blackouts on the sunlit side.

Bz

The north/south direction of the interplanetary magnetic field matters because southward Bz can couple more efficiently with Earth's magnetic field. Watch the trend rather than one instant reading.

KPC FIELD CHECK

What Does This Mean For My Activation?

Green-light setup

Quiet Kp, no significant R/S/G warning, decent solar flux, and bands that are producing signals when you listen. Set up, call CQ and see what happens.

Be flexible

Elevated Kp or active solar-wind conditions do not mean “stay home.” They mean try different bands, different directions, and shorter expectations for a single band.

Higher bands

When solar activity is supportive, check 17m, 15m, 12m and 10m instead of automatically staying on 20m. Higher bands can produce the fun surprise opening.

Storm conditions

If geomagnetic activity is strongly elevated or an R/S/G warning is active, expect more variability. Regional paths may still work even when a long-distance path is poor.
