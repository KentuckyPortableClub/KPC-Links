GET YOUR LICENSE

From zero to on the air: which license to start with, how to study for free, where to take the test in Kentucky, and what happens after you pass.

THREE LICENSE CLASSES

Which License?

Every license is good for 10 years. No Morse code test. You can take more than one element at the same session.

START HERE

Technician

Local VHF/UHF (2 m, 70 cm) — repeaters, simplex, APRS, fox hunts — plus some HF: CW on 80/40/15 m and voice on part of 10 m.

EXAM
35 questions, pass with 26
POOL
2026–2030 pool (since July 1, 2026)
FOR POTA
Great on 2 m and 10 m; HF CW too
MOST HF

General

Most of the HF bands for voice, CW and digital — where most POTA and SOTA activity happens.

EXAM
35 questions, pass with 26 (needs Technician)
POOL
2023–2027 pool
FOR POTA
The sweet spot — 20 m and 40 m SSB, FT8, CW
EVERYTHING

Amateur Extra

All amateur privileges, including the extra slices at the bottom of the bands where DX hangs out.

EXAM
50 questions, pass with 37 (needs General)
POOL
2024–2028 pool
EXTRAS
Can become a VE and give exams

STEP BY STEP

From Zero to On the Air

  1. Study (free)

    Most people pass the Technician in a few weeks of short sessions. Practice tests use the real question pool, so when you're scoring in the 80s you're ready.

    HAMSTUDY.ORG ↗

  2. Get an FRN from the FCC

    Your FCC Registration Number — free, takes five minutes, and you need it before the exam. Register in the FCC's CORES system.

    FCC CORES ↗

  3. Find an exam session

    Volunteer Examiner teams hold exams all over Kentucky, and some test online by video. Many charge about $15; some are free.

    HAMSTUDY SESSIONS ↗ARRL EXAM SEARCH ↗

  4. Pass, then pay the FCC

    After you pass, the FCC emails you a link to pay the $35 application fee. Pay within 10 days or the application is dismissed. Upgrades don't have a fee.

  5. Look yourself up

    Your call sign usually shows up in the FCC database within a few days of paying. Once it's there, you're legal to transmit.

    FCC LICENSE SEARCH ↗

  6. Get on the air with KPC

    Join the club, find a park, and make your first contacts. We'll help you with a radio, an antenna and your first activation.

QUESTIONS WE HEAR

License FAQ

Do I need to know Morse code?

No — the code test went away in 2007. Plenty of people learn it anyway because it's fun and great for portable work. Try our CW Practice page.

How old do you have to be?

There's no age limit. Kids pass the Technician all the time.

Can I take Technician and General the same day?

Yes. If you pass one element, most teams let you try the next one at the same session.

What's a vanity call sign?

A call you pick (if it's available) instead of the next one in line. It's applied for through the FCC and has the same $35 fee. Clubs can hold a club call too.

Can I listen without a license?

Yes — anyone can listen, use a WebSDR, or hunt a fox. You need a license to transmit.

How long does it last?

Ten years. You can renew online (with the $35 fee) — and there's a two-year grace period if you forget.
