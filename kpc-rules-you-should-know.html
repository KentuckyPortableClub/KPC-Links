SHOULD KNOW.

Three rulebooks apply every time you operate from a park: your license, the program, and the land you’re standing on. Here’s what matters in Kentucky.

01 • YOUR LICENSE

FCC Basics

The same rules you follow at home apply in the field.

✓ Identify your stationGive your callsign at least every 10 minutes during a contact and at the end of it.
✓ Stay inside your privilegesOnly transmit on the bands, modes and power your license class allows.
✓ You are the control operatorA licensed operator must be in control of the station whenever it transmits.
✓ No business, no musicAmateur radio isn’t for commercial use, and you can’t broadcast music or obscured messages.

02 • THE PROGRAM

POTA Rules

The short list. The official rules page has the full details.

✓ 10 contacts, one UTC dayTen or more QSOs from one park in the same UTC day makes a valid activation.
✓ Inside the boundary, on public landYou and all your equipment must be inside the park boundary and on public property — never on private land, even if the park surrounds it.
✓ Vehicles are fineA vehicle parked on public property inside the boundary is a legal operating position.
✓ Any band, any modeAll bands and modes your license allows, including WARC. Use only the power you need.
✕ No repeatersContacts through land repeaters don’t count.
✕ No relaysA third station can’t relay callsigns, reports or park numbers — both stations must hear each other.
✕ No fully automated contactsBoth operators must take direct action to make each contact.
✓ 2-fers need overlapFor two parks at once, stand where both boundaries overlap and keep a separate log for each park.

03 • WHERE YOU STAND

Kentucky Land Rules

Every kind of public land in Kentucky has its own rules. These are the ones portable operators run into most.

KENTUCKY STATE PARKS

State Parks

  • Trails are open sunrise to sunset — stay on designated trails.
  • Pets must be leashed. Pets are not allowed on trails dedicated as state nature preserve land — including most hiking trails at Natural Bridge (except Henson Arch and Whittleton) and the McCreary County side of Cumberland Falls.
  • No carving or damaging rock formations or trees — don’t tie off to anything that could be damaged.
  • Alcohol is not allowed on hiking trails unless posted otherwise.
  • Picnic shelters and campsites are often reserved — set up somewhere that doesn’t block other visitors.

KENTUCKY OFFICE OF NATURE PRESERVES

State Nature Preserves & Natural Areas

  • Open sunrise to sunset. Some sites are open only for research — check the location page first.
  • Trails are foot traffic only. Don’t shortcut or reroute trails.
  • No pets, camping, picnicking or fires on state nature preserves.
  • No collecting, hunting or trapping on dedicated preserves.
  • No bikes, horses, climbing or motorized vehicles.
RADIO OPERATORS: READ THIS ONE
Nature preserves prohibit audio equipment and electronic noise devices. Plan on headphones or an earpiece, a small footprint, and nothing attached to trees.

KENTUCKY FISH & WILDLIFE

Wildlife Management Areas (WMAs)

  • Hunting comes first on WMAs. Check the season dates for the area before you go.
  • During firearm deer, elk and bear seasons, hunters and anyone with them must wear solid hunter orange on the head, back and chest. If you’re not hunting it isn’t required — wear it anyway.
  • Some areas need a user permit just to enter — including Peabody WMA (ages 16+) and Otter Creek Outdoor Recreation Area.
  • Vehicles stay on maintained roads open to the public; park in designated lots or beside the road without blocking a road or gate.
  • Camping only in designated areas. Stay out of areas signed closed — some have seasonal closures.
  • It is unlawful to cut trees, litter, or damage property or habitat in any way.
  • Planning a club outing? A group of 10 or more gathering on a WMA must apply to KDFWR at least 30 days ahead.

U.S. FOREST SERVICE

Daniel Boone National Forest & Red River Gorge

  • Parking overnight on national forest land in the Red River Gorge requires an overnight parking pass displayed in your vehicle — sold at Forest Service offices and local vendors.
  • Back-country camping in the Red River Gorge is free with no permit; Big South Fork requires its own back-country permit.
  • Kentucky’s rule against nails, screw-in devices or wire in trees (written for tree stands) covers WMAs and the Daniel Boone National Forest — never drive or wrap anything into a tree.
  • Check the forest’s alerts page for closures before you drive — roads and areas close after storms.

NATIONAL PARKS, REFUGES & OTHER FEDERAL LAND

NPS Sites & National Wildlife Refuges

  • Each site sets its own rules — national parks publish them in the Superintendent’s Compendium; refuges list them on their rules page.
  • Expect posted hours, closed areas, and limits on where you can set up. Refuges protect wildlife first — some areas close seasonally.
  • When in doubt, stop at the visitor center and ask. Rangers are usually glad to help.

BEFORE YOU LEAVE THE HOUSE

Rules Checklist

  • License privileges match the band you plan to use
  • Official boundary map checked — you’ll be on public land inside it
  • Park hours checked (many trails close at sunset)
  • Hunting seasons checked for WMAs and forests; orange packed
  • User permit or overnight parking pass, if the area needs one
  • Headphones packed for nature preserves
  • Antenna plan that doesn’t damage trees or block trails
  • Weather and lightning forecast checked
  • Someone knows where you’re going
  • Logging set to UTC time

Rules change — always check the land manager’s current rules and alerts before you go. Sources: POTA rules • FCC Part 97 • Kentucky State Parks trail guidelines • Kentucky Nature Preserves FAQ • KDFWR Public Lands Hunting Guide • Clarks River NWR rules
