  - R1 TWO JOBS.   - R3 THE SIMPLE TEST. Overlap (even a few feet) + public ground = 2-fer, the WHOLE overlap. Several PUBLIC owners on the patch = still a 2-fer. A PRIVATE piece is cut out and labelled 'private land'. Owner unknown in a database = look it up; only that piece stays not sure.
  - R4 LAYERED LAND. A federal POTA park + a state POTA park on that federal land (federal owns, state manages) = 2-fer IN THE OVERLAP.   - R7 WATER ONLY. When the overlap is only water, it is called 'water only' (boat / kayak). Boat rows are KEPT.

- **2-FER RULES FOR THE WHOLE WORLD (HQ + Brandon, Oct 27 4:29 AM: 'we need rules that apply to the rest of the world also'; the bot (kpc_fers.py) and the desk use these everywhere):** (1) THE PARK = WHERE ITS DESIGNATION LEGALLY APPLIES. (3) LAYERING IS THE NORMAL 2-FER: one designation on top of another on the same ground (WMA on national forest land, wilderness or wild river inside a forest, nature reserve inside a national park, owned by one agency and managed by another) = real. Two owners SIDE BY SIDE = touching, not a 2-fer. (4) SIZE (Brandon Oct 27 4:59 AM): 100+ yards across = main list; 50-100 yards = main list with the 'small overlap: tight fit, check carefully' label; 25-50 yards = a separate VERY TIGHT list, members only, labelled 'could be map error: check both official maps on the ground'; under 25 yards = not shown (official maps can be off 10-15 yards). 'Across' = a circle of that size fits inside the overlap in every direction (the bot shrinks the overlap by half the size from every edge), so long thin slivers never pass. (5) RIVERS + TRAILS (Brandon Oct 27 4:33 AM: 'wilderness rivers are not trails it is an overlap'): a river or trail park WITH an official corridor/area boundary (Kentucky Wild Rivers in ky-wild-rivers.geojson, US wild & scenic river corridors, trail corridors) = an AREA park: test it like any other park (rules 1-4: overlap of the corridor polygon and the other park, 50-yard patch, public + open land, corridor tracts are mixed owners so the spot must be on public land, e.g. Forest Service Basic Ownership or a state-owned tract). ONLY a river or trail park that is just a LINE (no official corridor: national historic/scenic trail centre lines, water trails) uses POTA item 5 = public land within 100 ft (30.5 m) of the trail line or river EDGE, inside the other park; water routes use the official shoreline. (6) SOURCES, best first: the managing agency's official boundary > the national protected-area database (US PAD-US, Canada CPCAD, EU Natura 2000/national registers; WDPA/Protected Planet only if its licence allows) > OpenStreetMap (never confirms a 2-fer alone) > POTA pins (never). Proclamation/purchase lines are not land. (7) NOT SURE when official sources cannot settle it: kept out of every guide/list, re-checked when data arrives; never a 'no'. (8) Always POTA only, always 'we think', both notices (CLAUDE.md), nothing public without Brandon's GO. ## RULE SHEET G: ONE BOUNDARY PIPELINE (chat 2, Fable, Oct 29 2026 12:10 PM CDT; RULES FIRST; Brandon 12:03 PM 'maps need to be accurate and the process smooth and efficient … how do we fix it?' → 12:05 'Yes' to the plan; DRAFT for chat 8 to break, then Brandon rules the grey areas, then HQ records, THEN chat 2 builds)
Why: today a wrong piece is caught five steps late (key → 2-fer line → hold → parcel check → three chats), names alone decide what is drawn, outlines are stored sharp but drawn rough, and every rerun is by hand. This sheet moves every check to the KEY (where the outline is made) and leaves the tools downstream nothing to guess.

**1. What counts as a park's outline (a piece joins the park only if ALL hold):**
- R-G1 OFFICIAL: the record comes from the managing agency's own map (or the federal/national layer for federal units; PAD-US only where the agency has no map). Never OSM, never a drawn guess.
- R-G2 NAME AGREES: the record's name is the park's name, an agreed alias (overrides.json), or the park's name with the layer's type word dropped (rule sheet C). The DESIGNATION words must agree (a Provincial Park never joins a Conservation Reserve record; a Wildlife Refuge never joins a National Forest).
- R-G3 LOCATION PROVES IT (the new rule, replaces 'name is enough'): the POTA pin is INSIDE the record, OR within 100 m of its edge, OR the record touches / lies within 2 km of a piece of the same park that already passed. A piece more than 2 km from every passed piece joins ONLY when the agency's own record carries the park's name AND a unit/tract field that ties it to the park (e.g. 'Daniel Boone NF, Redbird District'); otherwise it goes to the queue, not the map.
- R-G4 WATER: a lake-surface record joins only right at the pin (as today).
- R-G5 SPLIT PARKS: a park the agency splits into named units (Whitewater WMA: Main Branch Unit …) takes every unit whose name starts with the park's name within 30 km, once one unit holds the pin (alias_all, as today).

**2. What does not:**
- a same-named record far away with nothing tying it to the park (Little Sextons Creek WMA's 'far piece', the Lost Lakes of Washington);
- a record that is only PART of the park when the park has its own record (Beall Woods Nature Preserve inside Beall Woods SP joins as a PIECE and is labelled so, never as the whole);
- a bigger record that merely contains the pin (Coeur d'Alene Lake for Mica Bay Boater Park; a 298,000-ac BLM record for four small POTA parks; the Ouachita National Wildlife Refuge for the Ouachita National Forest);
- ONE record for TWO POTA numbers (Kaskaskia + West Okaw units of Shelbyville): both get 'pin only' until unit lines exist; shared records never make a 'same land' pair by accident;
- land POTA has retired (data/retired-refs.json): out of every file, read from that one list.

**3. Sources, best first:** (1) the managing agency's own layer on the state/provincial open-data portal; (2) the agency's ArcGIS Online account; (3) the national layer (NPS legal boundary, USFS, FWS realty, BLM, CPCAD); (4) PAD-US / a zip download from the state clearing house; (5) nothing → PIN ONLY. A layer that needs a login or token is dropped (Alberta trails) and its parks fall to the next source.

**4. What happens when unsure:** the piece does NOT go on the map. It goes to the WAITING ROOM with the evidence on ONE line (park, record name, pin distance, acres, owner, map link) and a yes/no question. One person answers once; the answer lands in overrides.json ('join' / 'no' / 'alias' with 'when' + 'why') and is never asked again. No chat-to-chat lists. The 110 held far rows + the 60 name-match 'not sure' rows are the first batch through it.

**5. What people see:** every outline carries ONE grade saved with it, read by every tool (2-fer line, Am I in the Park, cards, kit, app packs): SURE = official record(s), pin inside, every piece passed R-G1..G5 · APPROXIMATE = a person picked the closest official land (bigger / other shape) or a piece joined by alias within 100 m · PIN ONLY = no outline. Cards say 'Official outline', 'Approximate outline', or 'Pin only (no official outline yet)'. The site draws EXACTLY the stored outline when zoomed in (no second rounding); a simplified copy is used only zoomed out. The short information notice stays on every map (notices.md).

**6. The loop (smooth + efficient):**
- Monthly (the 5th, after the POTA list refresh): per layer ONE tiny request ('changed since we saved?': edit date / feature count / ETag). Unchanged = reuse the saved outline, no download. Changed = re-read that layer, re-grade only its parks, re-run only their 2-fer area. Forced full read once a quarter.
- One 'look at this' line per CHANGED area only (what joined, dropped, acres ±10%, grade changed, and why: 'agency edited its map'); unchanged areas = one line 'no change'.
- No temporary workflows, no hand dispatches except 'full' by Brandon's word. Red run = one email, only when a save failed or a layer broke twice.
- Acreage: exact union acres for every park; the big ones (> 60k points) are done one or two a night by a small job, never inside the rebuild.

2. THE AGENCY'S OWN RECORD FIRST: search ALL of the managing agency's layers and records for a piece that NAMES or IDs that park (a separate record, another layer, a named unit inside the big shape). 3. 4. BOUNDARY, THEN OWNERSHIP: (a) the park has its own boundary line on an official map → that line is the outline. 5. PUT IT IN THE RIGHT SPOT: chat 2 traces / cuts the park's piece out of the big shape so it follows the official line (R-G6: 3 m, all records merged). The rest of the big shape is untouched and keeps its own park.
6. CHECK BEFORE ADDING (G-4): chat 11 answers yes/no against the official map (right place, right size, right owner, same park), chat 2 rechecks, then it is added STAGED (freeze: mass dump). Unsure = pin + waiting room, asked once.

"The intersection must entirely contain the activator and the station equipment." OURS: two POTA-NUMBERED parks whose OFFICIAL boundaries overlap on PUBLIC ground (R0, R3). Touching at a border = not a 2-fer. Land with no POTA number never pairs.
2. HOW MANY PARKS AT ONCE? POTA: "two-fer, three-fer, etc." with no limit; "A separate log must be submitted for each park." OURS: a 3-fer = one patch where all three overlap (R: 3-fers share one patch).
3. WHERE MUST THE OPERATOR BE? POTA: "The activator and all equipment must be within the park's boundary and on public property." Straddling a line = "not fully within the boundary and ... out of compliance." OURS: the whole station inside the overlap; the overlap patch we draw is that ground (50-yard patch rule).
4. WHICH MAP DECIDES? POTA: "Use maps provided by Parks on the Air as guidelines only. Refer to official park websites and agencies to find the official boundaries." OURS: the managing agency's own map (R-G1); PAD-US only where the agency has none; OpenStreetMap never. POTA pins can be wrong (Brandon 12:14).
5. PRIVATE LAND INSIDE A PARK? POTA: "Activators may not attempt to activate from private property, even if the private property is adjacent to, or surrounded by, park property." "Activators may not trespass on private property to access ... lands." OURS: private inholdings are cut out of the park's outline and labelled 'private land' (R3); a national forest = only the land the agency owns, never the proclamation line (R4).
6. A TRAIL THAT IS ITS OWN PARK (no land boundary)? POTA: "If a trail system or a river is designated as a Parks on the Air entity by itself (not as parts of a land park having a defined boundary), the activator and the station equipment must be on public property within 30.5 meters (100 feet) from the edge of the trail or river." OURS: the trail park = a 100 ft strip each side of the official trail line, PUBLIC ground only; where that strip overlaps another POTA park's boundary on public ground = 2-fer (TRAIL rows, e.g. Channahon x I&M Canal).
7. A RIVER THAT IS ITS OWN PARK (no land boundary)? Same POTA sentence. OURS: 100 ft from the EDGE OF THE WATER (Brandon Oct 28: 'river trails 100 ft from edge of water'), public ground only. A river park WITH a defined boundary (a Wild River corridor, e.g. Kentucky's Green River US-10105) uses its OFFICIAL CORRIDOR boundary, not the 100 ft rule (POTA: 'not as parts of a land park having a defined boundary'); that corridor overlapping another park = 2-fer (Mammoth Cave x Green River WR = YES).
8. A WATER TRAIL / BLUEWAY PARK? POTA has no separate rule: it is a 'river ... designated ... by itself' → 100 ft from the water's edge on public ground (OURS, Brandon Oct 28).
9. A TRAIL OR RIVER RUNNING THROUGH A LAND PARK? POTA: no explicit rule beyond #6/#7. OURS: if the trail/river is its OWN POTA park, its strip (or corridor) overlapping the land park on public ground = 2-fer; a trail that is just a feature inside the park is not a park (R0).
10. ON THE WATER (boat, lake, shoreline)? POTA: no rule for boats; only "Activations from vehicles, RVs, etc., parked on public property within the park's boundary are permissible" and "Aeronautical activations are permitted if the QSOs are made from airspace directly above the park." OURS: inside the official boundary = inside the park, water included; an overlap that is ONLY water is listed APART as 'water only' (R7), never hidden, never called land. A lake record joins a park only right at the pin (R-G4).
11. A PARK ON TWO STATES? POTA: a log "should be for only one park in a single location (in case the park straddles multiple locations)"; "Location (Chosen at time of upload if park crosses state lines)." OURS: one park, one outline; the state line is not a park boundary; a 2-fer is judged the same on either side.
12. CAN A PARK BE INSIDE ANOTHER PARK OF THE SAME AGENCY? POTA: "POTA does not create sub-parks within existing parks if both parks are part of the same government tier or agency." OURS: so two POTA numbers of the SAME agency on the same land = 'same land, two park numbers', shown with that label (R: same land); a state park sitting on federal-OWNED land = 2-fer in the overlap (R4, R-SF); a state-OWNED park inside a federal outer line = hard boundary, not a 2-fer.
13. IS THE PARK OPEN? POTA: "A park is considered open when the public has civil and legal access, or a special permission/permit ... is obtainable." "A park is closed when the governing agency/website clearly says that public access is prohibited or when the park itself ceases to exist." Seasonal closures of parts "may still allow lawful access to the remainder." "If in doubt, please call the park office/administrator." OURS: a closure is a closure only when the agency's own words close land to the public (Brandon Oct 29 9:23: quota hunts = notes; 'Tract 1 is closed to public access' = a real closure of that part); hunting-season rules and bag limits are never closures.
14. WHICH PARKS EXIST? POTA: "All new parks must be owned and operated by the two (2) highest tiers of government"; parks with "a formal government website including a boundary map SHALL" have it on their POTA page. OURS: we never add or question a park (P0, R0); if POTA numbers it, we find its official boundary (R-G1..G7) and apply the rules above.
15. WHOSE RULES WIN? POTA: local/federal laws and licence conditions "supersede anything contained in these documents"; POTA is "a self-regulated, community-monitored program." OURS: honor system; every card carries the 2-FER notice ('Parks' boundaries may overlap here, but we can't confirm it ...') and the maps notice (notes/notices.md). We never say parks DO overlap.
16. PARKING LOTS, ROADS, PULL-OFFS (Brandon Oct 29 1:20 PM: 'Parking lots are ok if your on the property it is ok and publicly open'): a parking lot, road or pull-off INSIDE the park's official boundary, on the park's property and open to the public = inside the park (POTA: "Activations from vehicles, RVs, etc., parked on public property within the park's boundary are permissible"). A lot on a public road right-of-way or private land outside the line = not in the park; our access points say 'Nearby public access, not the park's own' for those. A lot inside the OVERLAP of two parks = a 2-fer spot like any other ground.
17. NOT ANSWERED BY POTA (ask Brandon as a numbered grey area, keep the row NOT SURE meanwhile): a trail park's strip where the official trail line itself is missing; county or city parks POTA has numbered that have no official map.
