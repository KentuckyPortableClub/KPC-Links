WHAT POTA ADDS ▸
  • Owned AND run by the federal or state government (no city, county, private, non-profit, commission or district parks).
  • Open to the public (hard access is fine; permit-only is not).
  • Not a sub-park inside a bigger park of the same agency.
  • Not cut off by private land (a public road, trail or water way in).
  • Not a Corps of Engineers site, heritage area, National Recreation Trail, historic property or tribal land.
  • Has an official agency page and boundary map.
  • Emails go to your state's Mapping Representative. POTA rules ↗
WHAT WWFF / KFF ADDS ▸
  • Nature related: no buildings, monuments, battleships, statues or old railroads.
  • Managed by a federal or state agency (no city parks or private centers).
  • Open to visitors.
  • Wildlife Management Areas only if major (over 500 acres).
  • Worldwide: a protected area (IUCN categories, Ramsar, biosphere reserve and similar, listed in the WDPA).
  • Emails go to the KFF coordinator. KFF rules ↗ · WWFF rules ↗
WHAT SOTA ADDS ▸
  • A distinct peak with at least 150 m (492 ft) of prominence.
  • Legal access to the top (open access or permission).
  • Operating rules: inside 25 m (82 ft) below the top, not in or next to a vehicle, no fixed power.
  • Your state's SOTA Association Manager handles new summits; most qualifying peaks are already listed.
  • SOTA General Rules ↗

It searches the national public land database (every federal and state agency in the state), the live National Park Service and Fish & Wildlife maps and OpenStreetMap, then checks POTA's and KFF's rules. The emails appear at the bottom of the result.

Or tap the top of the hill on the map (Topo and Terrain show the peaks). It checks the SOTA list, the height, who owns the land and the county, and writes the email to the state's SOTA Association Manager.
