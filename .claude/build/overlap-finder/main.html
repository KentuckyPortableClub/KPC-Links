Places where our maps suggest the boundaries of two or more POTA parks may overlap, checked by our rules and by a person. Pick a result to watch the park outlines draw in, see the spot, find the closest public access, and walk in with your GPS.

Please read: this is a research aid, not a ruling. The Park Overlap Finder shows places where our maps suggest the boundaries of two or more POTA parks may overlap. It is a starting point for your own research only. The Kentucky Portable Club cannot confirm or deny that any location listed here is a valid multi-park activation site. Park boundaries change, maps can be out of date or imprecise, and private land, closures and local rules may apply even inside a boundary. Before you count an activation, check each park's official boundary map from the managing agency, make sure you are on public land that is open to the public, and follow POTA's activation rules. POTA makes the final decision on every activation, not KPC. You are responsible for confirming your own location. KPC is an independent ham radio club, not part of Parks on the Air®.

    ▸ HOW WE CHECK (in plain words)
    • Official maps only: each park's own agency boundary first; national databases next; volunteer maps never confirm a spot.
    • A spot must fit a 50-yard circle inside every park (100+ yards = the main list; 50–100 = "small overlap: tight fit"; 25–50 = members only, "could be map error").
    • Only land the park really owns or manages counts, never private land inside a park line and never an outer "purchase" line. Two owners side by side is not a 2-fer.
    • A trail or river that is only a line uses POTA's 100-ft rule: the light band along the line IS the zone.
    • Water-only spots are listed apart (boat or kayak). Closures, seasons and hunting are noted.
    • Every result is checked by a person before it is listed. We try hard, and we can still be wrong. POTA decides, not KPC.
