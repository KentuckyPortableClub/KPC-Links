## US-0019: Cumberland Gap National Historical Park

🏔️ **US-0019 | Cumberland Gap National Historical Park**
Long before Kentucky was a state, this notch in the Cumberland Mountains was the "first doorway to the West." Native Americans used the trail for generations, and Daniel Boone's crew opened it to settlers. As the Wilderness Road, it carried an estimated 200,000–300,000 pioneers into Kentucky and the Ohio Valley before 1810! 🛤️
Today the park covers about 24,000 acres where Kentucky, Tennessee, and Virginia meet. Drive up to the Pinnacle Overlook for a three-state view, stand on Tri-State Peak, take a ranger tour of Gap Cave, or visit the mountaintop Hensley Settlement, an early-1900s farm community. With 85 miles of trails, there's plenty to explore. 🥾📻

## US-0050: Mammoth Cave National Park

🦇 **US-0050 | Mammoth Cave National Park**
Under the hills of south-central Kentucky lies the **longest known cave system on Earth**, with more than 426 miles surveyed and more still being found! 🤯
The cave's history goes deep. Miners dug saltpeter here for gunpowder during the War of 1812. Later, Stephen Bishop, an enslaved guide, explored and mapped miles of passages and became one of the cave's most famous figures. Mammoth Cave became a national park in 1941, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981, and an International Dark Sky Park in 2021. 🌌
Take a cave tour, paddle the Green River, hike the forest trails, or stay after dark to stargaze. A must-see for any Kentucky road trip! 🔦📻

## US-0295: Clarks River National Wildlife Refuge

🦆 **US-0295 | Clarks River National Wildlife Refuge**
Near Benton in western Kentucky, this refuge protects one of the largest remaining bottomland hardwood forests in the region. It was established in 1998 as the nation's 549th national wildlife refuge. 🌳
About 11,890 acres along the East Fork of the Clarks River hold bald cypress, tupelo gum, and big oaks, and more than 200 species of migratory birds stop here through the year. Look for wood ducks, warblers, and wintering waterfowl. 🐦
Visiting is free. Hike, bird, fish, paddle, or ride horseback, and start at the Environmental Education & Recreation Area for an easy walk. It's a quiet, swampy slice of the Jackson Purchase that's worth the trip. 📻

## US-0538: Reelfoot National Wildlife Refuge (KY/TN)

🦅 **US-0538 | Reelfoot National Wildlife Refuge**
This refuge exists because the earth shook! The New Madrid earthquakes of 1811–1812 created Reelfoot Lake, and today it's one of the richest wildlife spots in the Mississippi River basin. 🌊
The refuge was established in 1941 and covers 10,428 acres. It reaches north from Tennessee into Fulton County, Kentucky, where the Long Point Unit's observation tower looks out over thousands of ducks and geese in winter.
The big draw is eagles: about 100–200 bald eagles spend the winter here, and the refuge offers free guided eagle tours in January and February (reservations required). 🦆❄️
⚠️ Hams: access rules and dates are seasonal, so check before you go!

## US-0608: Ohio River Islands National Wildlife Refuge (KY/PA/WV)

🏝️ **US-0608 | Ohio River Islands National Wildlife Refuge**
Did you know Kentucky has a piece of this island refuge? Established in 1990, it protects islands and mainland tracts along 362 miles of the upper Ohio River. Kentucky's part is **Manchester 1 and Manchester 2 Islands** in Lewis County. 🛶
Early settlers called them "the Three Islands." The third one has since washed away into a gravel bar. Frontier history runs deep here: in the late 1700s, Native Americans sometimes used these islands to ambush pioneers floating down the river.
Today the islands are slowly returning to forest. They shelter migrating birds and 47 species of native freshwater mussels, 8 of them endangered. You can reach them by boat or kayak from the Manchester, Ohio ramp. 📻

## US-0686: Big South Fork National River and Recreation Area (KY/TN)

🌄 **US-0686 | Big South Fork National River & Recreation Area**
About 125,000 acres of Cumberland Plateau wilderness cut by the free-flowing Big South Fork of the Cumberland River! Expect deep gorges, sandstone bluffs, and one of the highest concentrations of natural bridges in the eastern U.S. 🪨
On the Kentucky side, in McCreary County, step back into coal-mining history at **Blue Heron (Mine 18)**. The Stearns Coal & Lumber Co. ran this company town from 1937 until the mine closed in 1962. It has been re-created as an outdoor museum, with the coal tipple still standing over the river. ⛏️
Hike, paddle, ride horses, or join the park's annual Blue Heron "Ghost Mine" event. Kentucky also protects the river as a state Wild River. 📻

## US-0703: Fort Donelson National Battlefield (KY/TN)

⚔️ **US-0703 | Fort Donelson National Battlefield**
February 1862 was a turning point! Gen. Ulysses S. Grant and Flag Officer Andrew Foote captured Confederate forts on the Tennessee and Cumberland Rivers. Those were the first major Union victories of the Civil War, and they made Grant a household name. Within days, Clarksville and Nashville fell. 🇺🇸
The main battlefield is in Dover, Tennessee. Kentucky's piece is **Fort Heiman** in Calloway County, a Confederate position in the Fort Henry fight that now overlooks Kentucky Lake. The park has preserved it since 2006.
Walk the earthworks and river batteries, visit the Dover Hotel where the surrender happened, and pay your respects at the national cemetery. 📻

## US-0724: Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park

🎩 **US-0724 | Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park**
Our 16th president was born right here in Kentucky, on February 12, 1809, in a one-room log cabin on Sinking Spring Farm near Hodgenville! 🇺🇸
A grand neoclassical Memorial Building now shelters a symbolic birth cabin. Theodore Roosevelt laid its cornerstone in 1909, and William Howard Taft dedicated it in 1911. Count the 56 steps up to the door: one for each year of Lincoln's life. There are also 16 windows for the 16th president. You can still see the "sinking spring" that gave the farm its name.
A few miles away is the park's second unit, **Knob Creek Farm**, Lincoln's boyhood home until age 7. Young Abe nearly drowned in that creek before a friend pulled him out! 📻

## US-1251: Carr Creek State Park

🏖️ **US-1251 | Carr Creek State Park**
A beach in the mountains of eastern Kentucky? Yep! Carr Creek State Park in Knott County, along KY Route 15 east of Hazard, has a 480-foot sand beach, the **longest beach in the Kentucky State Parks system**. ☀️
The park sits on Carr Creek Lake, a 750-acre reservoir the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built in 1976 on Carr Fork, a tributary of the North Fork of the Kentucky River.
Come for bass, crappie, and walleye fishing, rent a pontoon at the full-service marina, or camp at the 39-site campground. It's a relaxing summer stop in the heart of Appalachia. 🎣📻

## US-1252: Columbus-Belmont State Park

⚓ **US-1252 | Columbus-Belmont State Park**
In 1861, Confederate Gen. Leonidas Polk fortified these Mississippi River bluffs at Columbus and called the place the "Gibraltar of the West." To stop Union gunboats, he stretched a **massive chain across the river** to Belmont, Missouri. It was backed by 143 cannons. ⛓️
Floods in the 1920s exposed the chain. Today you can see its huge links and the 4–6 ton anchor, displayed in a stone monument built by the Civilian Conservation Corps. You'll also find the earthworks and the story of the "Lady Polk," a giant cannon that blew apart.
The museum, in a farmhouse that served as a Civil War hospital, opens seasonally. The river views alone are worth the drive! 📻

## US-1253: Dawkins Line Rail Trail State Park

🚲 **US-1253 | Dawkins Line Rail Trail**
This is **Kentucky's longest rail trail**! The line was built in 1912 by the Dawkins Lumber Company to haul timber out of the mountains, and the C&O took it over in 1930. After the tracks were abandoned in 2004, the state bought the corridor in 2011. The first 18 miles opened on June 15, 2013. 🛤️
Today the trail runs 36 miles from Hagerhill to Evanston through Johnson, Magoffin, and Breathitt counties. Along the way you'll cross **24 trestles** and pass through the Gun Creek Tunnel. Bring your bike, your hiking boots, or your horse, and settle in for a long day of Appalachian scenery. 🐎🌲📻

## US-1254: E.P. 'Tom' Sawyer State Park

⚽ **US-1254 | E.P. "Tom" Sawyer State Park**
Kentucky's big-city state park sits right in Louisville! It opened in 1974 on 550 acres that once belonged to Central State Hospital. The park is named for Jefferson County Judge/Executive Erbon Powers "Tom" Sawyer, who died in a 1969 car crash. He was the father of TV journalist Diane Sawyer. 📺
There's a lot packed in here: an Olympic-size pool, 14 soccer fields, a BMX track, an archery range, a dog park, and an airfield for model aircraft. After dark, the Louisville Astronomical Society runs its Urban Astronomy Center here. Stargazing and radio waves, all in one park! 🔭📻

## US-1255: Fishtrap Lake WMA

🎣 **US-1255 | Fishtrap Lake WMA**
Deep in the Pike County mountains, Fishtrap Lake fills a steep valley on the Levisa Fork. President Lyndon B. Johnson dedicated it in 1968. At 195 feet, its dam is the **highest in eastern Kentucky**. The 1,130-acre lake also supplies water to Pikeville. 💧
The wildlife management area surrounds the shoreline with rugged, almost completely forested hills. It's a quiet place for fishing, boating, hunting, and birding, with big mountain views at every bend in the road. 🦅⛰️
(Fishtrap was a Kentucky state park starting in 2003. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers has managed it since 2016.) 📻

## US-1256: Fort Boonesborough State Park

🪓 **US-1256 | Fort Boonesborough State Park**
This is where Kentucky really began! In April 1775, Daniel Boone's party crossed the Kentucky River and built a fort here. It became one of Kentucky's earliest European-American settlements. That May, the **first representative government in Kentucky** met at Boonesborough. In September 1778 the fort held out through the "Great Siege." 🏰
Today a reconstructed working fort brings the 1700s back to life, with resident artisans demonstrating pioneer crafts. Don't miss the Kentucky River Museum, and you can walk to the original fort site nearby, a National Historic Landmark. History lovers, this one is for you! 📻

## US-1257: General Burnside Island State Park

🏝️ **US-1257 | General Burnside Island State Park**
This is **Kentucky's only island state park**! The land was once called Point Isabel. In 1863, Union troops used it as "Camp Burnside," a supply base for Gen. Ambrose Burnside's East Tennessee campaign. ⚔️
It became an island after Wolf Creek Dam filled Lake Cumberland, and it joined the state park system in 1958. Today its 430 acres just south of Somerset offer camping, golf, and a boat ramp, all surrounded by Lake Cumberland water. Where else can you activate from an island in Kentucky? 🌊📻

## US-1258: Grayson Lake State Park

🛶 **US-1258 | Grayson Lake State Park**
In Carter and Elliott counties, the Little Sandy River was dammed in the 1960s to form Grayson Lake, which opened in 1969. The result is one of Kentucky's prettiest paddling lakes, lined with **sandstone cliffs from 30 to 200 feet tall**, little waterfalls, and the shady Grotto. Kentucky Tourism says the area was a favorite of the Shawnee and Cherokee. 🏞️
Paddle along the cliffs, hike the Lick Falls Trail or the Beech-Hemlock Forest Trail, play a round of golf, or set up camp by the water. 📻

## US-1260: John James Audubon State Park

🐦 **US-1260 | John James Audubon State Park**
The famous naturalist and bird artist John James Audubon lived in Henderson from 1810 to 1819 and studied the birds of these woods. 🎨
The park was built by the CCC and WPA starting in 1934. Crews built Wilderness Lake and, from local stone, the Audubon Museum (which tells his life story) and the Tea House, now the park office. The park has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1988, and most of it is a state nature preserve. Bring binoculars and walk the trails. Audubon would approve! 🦉📻

## US-1261: Kincaid Lake State Park

🎣 **US-1261 | Kincaid Lake State Park**
Near Falmouth in Pendleton County, this 850-acre park is built around 183-acre Kincaid Lake. The dam was finished in the early 1960s. For most of the 1960s the park was called Falmouth Lake State Park, and it went back to the Kincaid name in 1969. 🏞️
Kincaid is a **no-wake lake**, so it stays calm and peaceful, which makes it great for bass fishing and paddling. You'll also find 84 campsites, a 9-hole golf course, and a pool. It's a relaxing Northern Kentucky getaway! 📻

## US-1262: Kingdom Come State Park

⛰️ **US-1262 | Kingdom Come State Park**
High on Pine Mountain above Cumberland in Harlan County, this park takes its name from John Fox Jr.'s 1903 bestseller, *The Little Shepherd of Kingdom Come*. 📖
The star attraction is **Raven Rock**, a giant 290-foot rock face that slants up at a 45° angle. Also look for Log Rock, a natural sandstone bridge, plus Creech Overlook and a small mountain lake. A 225-acre nature preserve protects Indiana bats, and more than 3,000 of them winter in Line Fork Cave. Black bears live here too! 🐻
The mountaintop setting is great for radio. 📻

## US-1264: Levi Jackson Wilderness Road Park

🛤️ **US-1264 | Levi Jackson Wilderness Road Park**
Just south of London, two famous pioneer routes, **the Wilderness Road and Boone's Trace**, run right through this park. Settlers poured through here on their way into Kentucky. Levi Jackson's tavern was licensed in 1803, and his family's land was donated for a park in 1931. 🐴
Visit McHargue's Mill, built in 1939 with millstones that came over the Wilderness Road in 1812. At the Mountain Life Museum, the blacksmith shop played a jail in the 1955 Burt Lancaster movie *The Kentuckian*. 🎬
The Defeated Camp Burial Ground remembers the McNitt Company, killed here in 1786. There are 8.5 miles of trails to explore. 📻

## US-1274: Blue Licks Battlefield State Resort Park

⚔️ **US-1274 | Blue Licks Battlefield State Resort Park**

## US-3779: Big Bone Lick State Historic Site

🦣 **US-3779 | Big Bone Lick State Historic Site**
Ice Age giants once came here! **Mammoths, mastodons, and giant ground sloths** were drawn to the salty sulfur springs, and many got stuck and left their bones behind. Big Bone Lick is famous as the **"birthplace of American vertebrate paleontology."** 🦴
In 1755, Mary Draper Ingles was held captive here by Shawnee. She boiled brine for salt, then escaped and walked about 600 miles home. In 1807, **William Clark** dug up more than 300 bones here for President Thomas Jefferson.
Today you can tour the visitor-center museum, walk the trails, and see a live bison herd along the Bison Trace. 🦬📻

## US-3783: Jefferson Davis State Historic Site

🗼 **US-3783 | Jefferson Davis State Historic Site**
Rising 351 feet over the farmland at Fairview, this obelisk marks the **birthplace of Jefferson Davis**, the Kentucky-born president of the Confederacy. It's a striking reminder that Kentucky gave birth to **both** Civil War presidents, since Abraham Lincoln was born here too. 🏛️

The museum covers Davis's life and the monument's construction. Seasonal hours apply. 📻

## US-3785: Perryville Battlefield State Historic Site

⚔️ **US-3785 | Perryville Battlefield State Historic Site**
On **October 8, 1862**, one of Kentucky's most important Civil War battles was fought on these hills. Union Gen. Don Carlos Buell's army clashed with Confederate Gen. Braxton Bragg's. ## US-3788: Wickliffe Mounds State Historic Site

## US-3789: William Whitley House State Historic Site

🐎 **US-3789 | William Whitley House State Historic Site**
Near Crab Orchard stands **Sportsman's Hill**, the home of pioneer William Whitley. It is often described as **the first brick house built in Kentucky**. 🧱
Whitley loved horses, and his plantation had a racetrack that started American traditions. It's said to have had the **first clay track** in the U.S., and horses ran **counterclockwise**, unlike the British clockwise tradition. Every Kentucky Derby fan owes him a nod! 🏇
Whitley fought in the Kentucky militia, and at 64 he volunteered for the War of 1812. He was killed at the Battle of the Thames in 1813, and some accounts credit him with killing the Shawnee leader Tecumseh. A fascinating stop for history and horse lovers. 📻

## US-3790: Fort Heiman (Fort Donelson National Battlefield)

⚔️ **US-3790 | Fort Heiman**
High on a bluff above the Tennessee River (now Kentucky Lake), Confederate troops built Fort Heiman in the winter of 1861–62 to guard the river across from Fort Henry. It was named for Col. Adolphus Heiman, an architect who became a soldier. 🏰

## US-3791: Trail of Tears National Historic Trail (Kentucky segments)

🕯️ **US-3791 | Trail of Tears National Historic Trail**
This trail remembers one of the darkest chapters in American history. In 1838–39 the U.S. government **forced the Cherokee people from their homelands** in the Southeast to Indian Territory (now Oklahoma). Thousands of men, women, and children walked the Northern Route through western Kentucky in a bitter winter, and many did not survive. 
In **Hopkinsville**, the Trail of Tears Commemorative Park marks a winter camp and the graves of two Cherokee leaders, **Chief Whitepath and Fly Smith**, who died there in 1838. At **Mantle Rock** near Smithland, more than 1,000 Cherokee waited about two weeks for the frozen Ohio River to thaw. A trail there follows part of the historic road. 
These are places to walk slowly, learn, and remember. 🙏📻

## US-7708: Mill Springs Battlefield National Monument

## US-7950: Camp Nelson National Monument

## US-7967: Blanton Forest State Nature Preserve

🌲 **US-7967 | Blanton Forest State Nature Preserve**
Kentucky's largest old-growth forest! **Grover and Oxie Blanton** bought this land in 1928, and their family protected it from logging. In 1995 the first 1,075 acres were dedicated as a nature preserve. Today it covers about 3,500 acres in Harlan County. 🙌

## US-12752: Green River State Natural Area

⚔️ **US-12752 | Green River State Natural Area**
Big springs and Civil War history! The Rush Island Bottoms unit protects **Gorin Mill Springs**, reputedly the **largest spring in Kentucky**, and over a mile of Green River frontage near Munfordville. 💧
The **Battle of Munfordville** was fought here. On September 17, 1862, Confederate Gen. Simon Bolivar Buckner accepted the Union surrender. Historians call it a strategic high-water mark for the Confederacy in the West. The river here is a global **mussel-diversity hotspot**. Paddle downriver to see **Three Hundred Springs**, an 85-foot waterfall on private land. 🛶📻

## US-12971: Bat Cave State Nature Preserve

🦇 **US-12971 | Bat Cave State Nature Preserve**
A winter home for endangered bats! Dedicated in 1981 inside **Carter Caves State Resort Park**, Bat Cave protects a major winter roost of the federally endangered **Indiana bat**, with wintering numbers estimated at about **28,000**. 🌙
To protect hibernating bats, the **caves are closed from October to May**, so they're closed right now. The surface trails stay open year-round, though. Carter Caves also offers lodge stays, camping, and guided tours of other caves. Saltpetre Cave was mined during the **War of 1812** for gunpowder. 🥾📻

## US-12972: Cascade Caverns State Nature Preserve

🌿 **US-12972 | Cascade Caverns State Nature Preserve**
Ice-age plants in a Kentucky gorge! Dedicated in 1981 inside **Carter Caves State Resort Park**, this preserve protects two rare plants: **mountain maple** and **Canada yew**. 🌲
