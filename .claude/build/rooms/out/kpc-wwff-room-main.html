WWFF & KFF

WWFF is a worldwide program for nature areas. In the USA it's called KFF, and many Kentucky POTA parks also have a KFF number, so you can often do both on one trip.

WHAT IT IS

WWFF in a Minute

WWFF stands for World Wide Flora & Fauna. It's about operating from protected nature areas. Each country runs its own program; the USA's is KFF, and its references look like KFF-1277. It works a lot like POTA, but the numbers and logging are different.

THE RULES

📜 The Main Rules

Our plain-words summary of the WWFF Global Rules (KPC wrote it; it is not official). KFF (the US program) can have its own extra rules: check wwff.us.

  • 44 contacts count an activation toward WWFF activator awards. They don't have to happen in one visit: you can add them up over several trips to the same reference. National programs like KFF may accept fewer for their own awards.
  • All of your equipment must be inside the area's boundary. Part of the station inside isn't enough.
  • One reference at a time. Where two WWFF areas overlap, pick one; you can start a new activity under the other one after you finish the first.
  • Send in your log even if you don't reach 44. Log every contact (ADIF or the WWFF CSV template).
  • Activators don't upload their own logs to WWFF Logsearch. Logs go to the national log manager (for KFF, see wwff.us for how to send yours).
  • Hunters: every contact counts once the activator's log is in Logsearch, no matter how many contacts the activator made. No QSL cards needed.
  • Same ground as a POTA park? A KFF reference sharing ground with a POTA park is a different program: you can log both on one trip, but that's not a POTA 2-fer.

Sources: WWFF Global Rules v5.10 and How to activate a WWFF reference.

These are short, plain-language summaries to get you started. The program's own rules always win: read the official pages linked here before you go, because rules can change.

KPC is an independent ham radio club. We are not part of WWFF or KFF and this is not official WWFF material: the official sites are wwff.co and wwff.us.

HOW TO START

🚀 Your First KFF Activation or Hunt

📻 Activating

  1. Find a KFF reference: KY Park Commander, our KFF guide, or the Info Finder for other states.
  2. Check the boundary with Am I in the Park? (it shows WWFF areas too).
  3. Many Kentucky KFF areas are also POTA parks: log both programs at once.
  4. Spot yourself with Spot Me (fill in the WWFF/KFF box) and call CQ.
  5. Aim for 44 over one or more visits, then send your log the way wwff.us explains.

🎯 Hunting

  1. Watch the WWFF spots (Spot Me and Happening Now link to them).
  2. A POTA activator in a park with a KFF number is often activating KFF too: ask, or check the spot.
  3. Log the contact with the KFF reference number.
  4. Your credit shows up in WWFF Logsearch once the activator's log is uploaded.
