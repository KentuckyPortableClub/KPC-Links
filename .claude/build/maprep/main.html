📜 THE RULES YOU AGREED TO · POTA MAPPING REPRESENTATIVE AGREEMENT (REV. SEPT 1 2026), IN PLAIN WORDS

Your job is to apply the published rules the same way everywhere, not to find a way to make a park qualify. Sometimes the right answer is no, even for a friend or your club: that is the standard talking, not you. Unclear or unusual? Ask your Mapping Representative Lead; you are not expected to solve it alone.

THE REFERENCES, IN ORDER

  1. Qualification of Parks and Eligible Areas for Parks on the Air: the controlling eligibility policy. It is sent to Mapping Reps by the program (not on the public docs site as of Oct 2026): keep your copy handy and ask your Lead for the current revision.
  2. Country Eligibility Schedule: which governmental tiers qualify in each country (US: federal and state; Canada: federal and provincial/territorial). Also sent by the program; ask your Lead for the current copy.
  3. Knowledge Bank and operating guidance: the practical cases. Park Additions and Edits (docs.pota.app) ↗ · POTA Rules ↗ · Glossary ↗

BEFORE YOU ADD A PARK, CONFIRM ALL FIVE (§4)

  • It is in a qualifying governmental tier for the country (US: federal and state; Canada: federal and provincial/territorial; never add from a tier that is not listed).
  • Ownership and operation meet the requirements. No exceptions, no local rulings.
  • It is open to the public (normal agency rules, permits, hours and seasons are fine).
  • It has identifiable boundaries backed by authoritative documentation.
  • There is an official government source: an agency web page, or, where one is not routinely available, government GIS data, agency maps or other official documents.
✅ COUNTS AS EVIDENCEThe agency's own page · state or federal GIS data (USGS PAD-US, state GIS, NPS, FWS) · agency maps and brochures · official documents. On this page these links carry a green OFFICIAL tag.
ℹ️ BACKGROUND ONLY, NEVER EVIDENCETourism pages · social media · commercial directories · Wikipedia · OpenStreetMap · web searches. Useful for leads, but a park is never added on them alone. Dashed UNOFFICIAL tag here.

NEVER (§3, §4, §7)

  • Never add from a governmental tier that is not listed for the country.
  • Never make an exception to the ownership or operation rules.
  • Never use an existing POTA park as precedent for adding another ("they have one, so…").
  • A designation, classification, inventory or registry listing never qualifies an area by itself; the land must pass every rule on its own.
  • Never deactivate an existing park because of an eligibility question: escalate it to your Lead. Temporary or seasonal closures are not deactivations; ordinary deactivation is for a park that no longer exists or was permanently closed by its agency.

ADDING THE PARK IN POTA.APP (§6)

  1. Open New Park in pota.app ↗ (sign out and back in after your rights are granted).
  2. Country/entity and the location for your jurisdiction.
  3. The official park name and the closest existing park type (never a new type). The tool suggests one in the facts box.
  4. GPS in decimal form (the COPY GPS buttons give that).
  5. The authoritative official source plus any access or activation notes that are needed.
  6. Comments only for what is really useful or unusual.
  7. Review, SAVE; the number is assigned by the database (the pin may take a while to show).

TALKING TO PEOPLE (§5)

Explain briefly, clearly and kindly why a park was or was not added. You do not have to debate the rules: rule and policy questions go to help@parksontheair.com; questions needing interpretation go to your Mapping Representative Lead first.
