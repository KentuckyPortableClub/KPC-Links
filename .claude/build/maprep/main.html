HOW TO USE THIS PAGE (NEW REPS START HERE)
  1. Find new parks first. At the top, pick the state or province and tap FIND NEW PARKS IN THIS PLACE. Every place gets checked by itself; open the green “Possible candidate” ones, read the checks and the official record, and copy the summary. Found a place yourself? Type its name or drop a pin and test it.
  2. Pick the state or province in CHECK A PLACE under the map.
  3. Search a name or drop a pin. Type a park name, a park number such as US-2954, or coordinates. Or tap the map to drop a pin.
  4. Read the official record card first. A US place shows its PAD-US record; a Canadian place shows its CPCAD record. Each code is spelled out, with a short hint and where the record comes from.
  5. Read the rules checklist. Pass, fail or unknown, and each line names the field it used. Unknown means you still need to check it.
  6. Run CHECK ALL IN THIS PLACE to compare the whole published list for that state or province against POTA’s list.
  7. Open the official links: the agency’s own page and map, and the record link. They always win over this summary.
  8. Read “Research on this place” for notes the club has already gathered on that place.
  9. The limited-maps note means that place has few live maps or saved outlines here, so lean harder on the agency’s own map.

FIND, CHECK AND TEST PARKS · ONE MAP

Find new parks to add

Pick a state or province and the Tool Box reads the official list of public and protected land (USGS PAD-US for a US state, Canada’s CPCAD for a province), then checks every place for you: is it already a POTA park (the same official record in our answer key, a park outline over it, a POTA pin inside it, or a park with the same name nearby), the nearest POTA park, any overlap with a park (part of a park, or a 2-fer), and the full official record. Everything shows on the one map below, and the details open in the panel next to it.

Checks the largest official places in the whole state or province (up to 150). It takes a minute or two; the status below shows each step.

Type an address or place (or GPS numbers), or tap the map to drop a pin. SEARCH checks the official land within about 6 km of that spot.

Addresses are turned into GPS with OpenStreetMap Nominatim, one lookup per search (© OpenStreetMap contributors).

Tap the map to drop a pin (Street View, directions and the rules check for that spot open in the panel). Drag the pin to move it. Green is a possible candidate, orange needs a person, brown is probably already a park, grey is already a park. Street map: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

LAYERS YOU CAN TURN ON

CHECK A PLACE · CHECK ALL IN THIS PLACE

Type a park name, a park number such as US-2954, or coordinates. Or drop a pin on the map. The place comes from the pin, from the name (“Fall Creek Falls, TN”), or from the menu. A name alone searches every loaded region. Official information is listed first, then POTA status, then club notes.

SEARCH THE OFFICIAL LAYERS (MAPPING DESK)

The United States, Canada, Great Britain, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand. Pick a place, or leave it blank to search every loaded region by name. No drawing tools. A lead export is a candidate list, not a boundary.

Pin vs this uses the pin on the map and does not need a name. Parks you could add lists public land near that pin that is not already a POTA park. Export leads downloads that list. It does not download outlines.
