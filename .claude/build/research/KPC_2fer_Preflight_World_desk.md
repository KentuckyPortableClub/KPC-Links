## 1. What the old world run (47,219 candidates) looks like (A)
- `data/fers/W-*.json` hold 59,193 live rows; **FR 44,587, PL 7,035, ES 3,203, GB 853, SE 749, AU 528, IT 524, FI 491, HR 325** (everything else under 200). Germany has 25 rows, Japan 0, Hungary 0.
- **Every country except AU is "saved outline only"** (no fresh check) apart from 333 of 853 UK rows: the pass-2 report says 16,740 of 38,822 parks had a saved copy only, and the pass-2 problem list is the EEA service `CDDA_Dyna_WM/MapServer/4` returning HTTP 502 (Bad Gateway) over and over. So the world run never actually re-read Europe's national layers.
- **No ownership, no closures, no water split, no access pin on any row:** `ownership: none`, `access_pin: none` on all 44,587 French rows and every other country. The pin source is US Census roads, which do not exist outside the US.
- **France is a designation stack.** The French rows list 2, 3 and 4 parks each (23,380 pairs, 14,375 triples, 6,832 quads), most of them the SAME land in several layers (a regional park + a regional reserve + a "biological reserve" + a Natura 2000 site). The script header says POTA's **10,400 French "Biological Reserve" parks are ZNIEFF** wildlife inventory zones (INPN). 40,339 name entries are Natura 2000. The median patch is 68.5 acres but the largest is **11.8 million acres**: "Mers Celtiques / Talus du Golfe de Gascogne" (a sea site) is on 69 rows over 100,000 acres.
- 29,746 French rows were already dropped because a pin sits outside its own outline, 585 UK rows likewise.

## 4. Park types that look like "agency-land" (rule 1) but have no ownership layer in the key
National or state forests (France ONF, UK Forestry England / FLS / NRW, Poland Lasy Państwowe, Spain montes públicos, Sweden Sveaskog, Finland Metsähallitus, Germany Landesforsten, Australia state forests) and army lands (Poland, UK MoD ranges, Germany Truppenübungsplätze). ## 6. QUESTIONS FOR BRANDON
- **W-1 (France ZNIEFF and other inventory-only designations).** POTA counts 10,400 ZNIEFF zones as parks, but they have no legal protection and are mostly private land. Rule 1 says "the park = where the designation legally applies", rule 2 says public land. **Rec:** ZNIEFF pairs are NOT SURE (kept out) unless the other park is public land with an owner; France shows only stacks involving real protected sites (regional parks, national parks, reserves, Natura 2000) on land the state owns.
- **W-2 (whole-line parks over private land).** National / nature parks and Natura 2000 sites are mostly private: the spot must be public and open. **Rec:** with no ownership data a pair is NOT SURE and kept out; show only (a) pairs whose spot sits on a public-land source we have (Australian Crown land, England/Scotland open-access land, state forests with a layer), with the SHORT notice, and (b) a members' "possible, no ownership data" list if Brandon wants one.
- **W-3 (right-to-roam countries: NO, SE, FI, Scotland, partly DE and England's open access).** The right to walk on private uncultivated land is not public land. **Rec:** it does NOT make private land public for rule 2; only land the state or a public body owns counts. Say "check you may be there".
- **W-4 (sea sites).** A spot over a marine polygon is water only. **Rec:** all marine and lake-surface patches go in the water-only list; the 11.8 million-acre French sea sites are never shown on land.
- **W-5 (licences).** KPC is a non-commercial club, so CC BY-NC and non-commercial layers (WA state forests, Japan FY2010) can be used with attribution; **WDPA is not used** (its terms bar redistribution). **Rec:** yes, and chat 2 adds a one-line licence per source (EEA, INPN, BfN, Natural England, CAPAD) to the data notes before storing any copy in the public repo.
- **W-6 (pairs from different layers of the same country).** France shows 14,000 triples because three layers describe the same land. **Rec:** list one row per patch, naming all parks on it (rule 11), and cap the public list per country at the biggest 200 until Brandon looks at a sample.
- **W-7 (countries not in the key).** New Zealand (if top 10): probe DOC's layer first.
