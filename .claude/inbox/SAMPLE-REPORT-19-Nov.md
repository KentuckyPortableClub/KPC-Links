# The 300-park sample: what it says (report of 20 Nov 2026, 5:25 PM Central)

By Fable. Recounted today from the private answer file in KPC-Store; the counts match the score sheet. Counts only: no sample park is named here or anywhere public.

## What was done

- 300 parks picked at random from the US parks that have an outline (about 11,585 of 13,118 US parks; the other 1,533 are pins).
- The key they were picked from: the build of 19 Nov. No US key build has run since, so the numbers describe the map as it is today.
- One outside grader checked each park against official sources only (agency pages, agency acreage reports, agency map layers). One grader, one pass: nobody has double-checked its answers.
- 8 parks with a known answer were hidden in the batch to test the grader. It got 6 of 8 right and named the planted wrong record 4 times of 4. One of the two misses may be a fault in our own answer, not the grader's.

## The result

| Verdict | Parks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Right, nothing wrong found | 211 | 70% |
| Holds a record that is not the park | 15 | 5.0% |
| Too big by size, no wrong record named | 7 | 2.3% |
| Pieces missing | 24 | 8% |
| Cannot be told | 43 | 14% |
| Total | 300 | |

- **The error rate: 15 of 300 = 5.0% hold land that belongs to something else.** With a sample this size the true rate is between 3.1% and 8.1%.
- Across all outlined US parks that is about 580 parks, somewhere between 360 and 940.
- Any fault at all (wrong land, too big, or pieces missing): 46 of 300 = 15%.
- Leaving out the 43 that could not be told: 82% right, 5.8% wrong land.
- All 15 wrong parks carry the grade "official" on our map. They sit in 10 states.

## Where the wrong land comes from

- The 15 parks hold 24 wrong records between them.
- 21 of the 24 were joined by NAME. 3 came another way.
- 12 of the 24 come from the national layer, 12 from state layers.
- Per record, the national layer is wrong about 5 times as often as a state "owned" layer (2.7% against 0.5%).

## Why 43 could not be told

Trails and parkways with no acres on our side, parks whose agency publishes no size, and agency sites that refused the grader (Louisiana, Nebraska, Idaho and others). Spread over 26 states. These are not counted as right or wrong.

## The busiest parks (a second list, not random)

353 of the 1,193 busiest parks were checked, mostly by size alone: 280 right (79%), 37 with pieces missing, 33 too big (9%), 3 cannot tell. This is not an error rate: the list is not random and wrong records were not looked for.

## What the fixes in hand would catch, measured on this sample

- **Size check** (0.33 to 3 times the official size, as in draft #306): catches 5 of the 24 parks the grader called wrong or too big, and flags 4 parks it called right. A published size was found for 82% of the sample.
- **National-layer rule** (job 4, on main as a report; run on the sample today): catches 2 of the 24 wrong records, on 2 of the 15 parks, and flags 2 right records of 419.
- **Nine saved "no" answers** (#305): nine known parks, fixed at the build.
- The two switched-off rules (leased land, pieces): 27 parks across the whole key. Not counted on the sample.
- **Not yet counted: how many of the 15 wrong parks all the fixes catch together.** From the two numbers above it is a minority. That count should be made before the build, so nobody expects more from the build than it can give.

## What it means

- About 7 in 10 outlines are right, 1 in 20 shows another place's land, and 1 in 7 cannot be checked from official sources.
- The cause is the name join. Neither size nor the national-layer rule alone catches most of it.
- The test of the build is the same sample method again on the new key: 300 fresh parks, one grader. Better than 5.0% = progress. Not better = stop.

## Limits of this report

- One grader, one pass. The second look at the rows it called wrong was never done.
- 14% unknown is large: the true wrong rate among those 43 is not known.
- The sample covers the US only. Canada and the world have never been sampled.
