UNUNS, BALUNS & CHOKES

Wind your own end-fed transformer, 9:1 unun or coax choke for a few dollars. Which core, how many turns, how to wire it, and how to test it before you take it to the park.

Which One Do You Need?

All of these are coils of wire on a ferrite ring ("toroid"). What changes is the ratio and how they're wired.

END-FED HALF-WAVE

49:1 or 64:1 Unun

Matches the very high impedance (~2,500–3,500 Ω) at the end of a half-wave wire down to 50 Ω. One wire, no tuner, works on 40/20/15/10 m. The POTA favorite.

RANDOM WIRE

9:1 Unun

Brings a "random" length wire into range of your tuner. Needs a tuner and a counterpoise, but works on almost any band with one wire.

EVERY ANTENNA

1:1 Choke / Balun

Stops RF from flowing back down the outside of your coax — the cause of RF in the shack, hot mics, noisy receive and odd SWR. Put one on dipoles, verticals and end-feds.

LOOPS & OCF

4:1 Balun

For off-center-fed dipoles and some loops (~200 Ω). Best built as two 1:1 chokes: inputs side by side, outputs in series (a "Guanella" current balun).

BUILD PLANNER

Plan Your Build

Pick what you're building and how much power you run. You get the core, turns, wire, parts and a test resistor value.

Ratings here are conservative starting points. Always do the heat test (below) before a long run — a hot core means go bigger.
HOW IT CONNECTS

Wiring Diagrams

Each "turn" is one pass of wire through the center hole of the core. Count passes, not wraps around the outside.

EFHW 49:1 (2 : 14)

Tapped Autotransformer

One wire, 14 passes through the core. Pull out a little loop after turn 2 and scrape it — that's the tap. Start → coax shield and counterpoise. Tap → coax center. End → antenna wire. The 100 pF capacitor goes across the coax connector. (64:1: same thing with 16 turns.)

9:1 UNUN

Three Wires Twisted (Trifilar)

Twist three colors together and wind them as one — 9 passes. Then join them in a chain: A start → shield and counterpoise. A end + B start → coax center. B end + C start → joined to each other only. C end → the wire. The coax sees one wire, the antenna sees all three: 9× the impedance.

4:1 BALUN (GUANELLA)

Two Cores, Two Wires Each

Wind two cores the same: two wires side by side, 10 passes. On the radio side the cores are in parallel: both A's → coax center, both B's → shield. On the antenna side they're in series: core 1 B joins core 2 A, and the two leftover ends are your balanced outputs.

1:1 CHOKE

Coax Through a Toroid

No separate wire — the coax is the winding. Pass it through the hole 8–14 times (see the recipes below), spread evenly around the ring, and keep the two ends apart. Connectors on both ends and it goes in-line anywhere.

Dots mean wires are joined (solder them). Lines that cross without a dot don't touch. Colors are just to tell the wires apart — use any colors you have.

MORE RATIOS

Other Transformers You Might Need

Less common, but they come up as soon as you try receive antennas, loops or random wires.

RECEIVE ANTENNAS · LoG, BEVERAGE, K9AY

Isolation Transformer (3 : 9 turns)

Two separate windings that never connect: 3 turns to the coax, 9 turns to the antenna. That's a 9:1 impedance step-up, and the isolation keeps coax-shield noise out. Receive only, so thin magnet wire on a small mix 73 or 43 core is plenty. Turns helper

RANDOM WIRES & VERTICALS · ~200 Ω

4:1 Unun

Two wires side by side, 10–12 turns (bifilar), like the 9:1 but with two wires:

  • A start → coax shield / counterpoise
  • A end + B start → coax center
  • B end → the antenna wire

Use it on a vertical or a wire that's around 200 Ω. It's not for balanced antennas. For an OCF dipole or loop, use the 4:1 Guanella above.

DIPOLES · 1:1

Bifilar Current Balun

Same job as the coax choke, but with screw terminals for the dipole legs. Wind 10–12 bifilar turns on an FT-240-31 (FT-140-43 for QRP):

  • Wire A: coax center → one dipole leg
  • Wire B: coax shield → the other leg

Use twisted pair or PTFE wire so the two wires stay close and evenly spaced.

DELTA & QUAD LOOPS · ~100 Ω

2:1 Match

The easiest 2:1 has no core: a quarter-wave of 75 Ω coax. The loop calculator gives the length. Commercial 2:1 ununs also work. Either way, add a 1:1 choke after it.

WHERE IT GOES

Dipoles & Verticals

Right at the feedpoint, where the coax meets the antenna. A second choke at the radio end helps with RF in the shack.

END-FEDS

EFHW Antennas

The coax shield is part of an end-fed's counterpoise. Put the choke a short way down the coax — about 0.05 wavelength (roughly 7 ft on 40 m) from the transformer — or at the radio end.

NO FERRITE?

Air-Wound "Ugly Balun"

6–8 turns of coax about 6" across, taped together. It only works well on one or two bands and is weaker than ferrite — fine in a pinch.

THE KNACK

Winding Tips

  • Use magnet wire. Enameled copper ("magnet wire") is standard. Scrape or sand the enamel off the ends — or use solderable enamel and a hot iron — then tin them.
  • Protect the wire. Ferrite edges can nick enamel. A wrap of PTFE (plumber's) or Kapton tape on the core first helps on bigger builds.
  • Count passes through the hole. Every time the wire goes through the center, that's one turn. Mark the start with tape so you don't lose count.
  • Spread turns evenly. Cover about ⅔–¾ of the ring and leave a gap between the start and end, where the voltage difference is highest.
  • Twist bifilar/trifilar wires first. Twist 3–4 twists per inch before winding so the wires stay together. Use different colors (or mark the ends) so you can tell A, B and C apart.
  • Pick the right capacitor. EFHW: 100–150 pF across the coax connector, rated 1 kV for QRP and 3 kV or more at 100 W. Ceramic or silver-mica. It flattens SWR on the high bands.
  • Box it. A small project box, an SO-239 or BNC, and stainless bolts or binding posts for the wire and counterpoise. Add an eye-bolt for strain relief so the wire tension pulls on the box, not the winding.
  • Seal it. A dab of hot glue or silicone holds the core still. Drill a tiny drain hole at the bottom for condensation.
  • Careful at the end of an EFHW. The far end of the wire (and the transformer output) runs at high voltage — hundreds to thousands of volts at 100 W. Keep it out of reach while transmitting.
BEFORE THE PARK

Testing Your Build

1 · RESISTOR TEST

Dummy Load Check

Put a resistor equal to 50 × the ratio across the output and check SWR on each band with an analyzer or NanoVNA:

  • 49:1 → 2,450 Ω (2.4 kΩ works)
  • 64:1 → 3,200 Ω (3.3 kΩ)
  • 9:1 → 450 Ω (470 Ω)
  • 4:1 → 200 Ω

Use plain carbon or metal-film resistors, low power only. Under 1.5:1 across your bands is a good build.

2 · HEAT TEST

Is the Core Big Enough?

Hook it to the real antenna, send a steady carrier (or FT8) for 30–60 seconds at your normal power, then feel the core. Warm is OK; hot means losses — use a bigger core, a second core, or less power.

3 · CHOKE TEST

Measure the Choke

With a NanoVNA, connect the coax shield ends of the choke across the analyzer (S21 "through" or series measurement) and read the impedance across your bands. Over 1,000 Ω is working; over 3,000 Ω is great.
