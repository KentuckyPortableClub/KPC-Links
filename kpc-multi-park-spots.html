Kentucky POTA Multi-Park Spots

Places in Kentucky where you may be able to work two or three POTA parks at once, with GPS guide pins that get you to the area, checked against the POTA rules: overlapping boundaries on land that belongs to both parks, and official trail lines (Sheltowee Trace, Pine Mountain Trail, Trail of Tears, Lewis & Clark) running through other parks.

GPS PINS ARE GUIDES ONLY  •  DO YOUR OWN RESEARCH  •  CONFIRM EVERY BOUNDARY ON THE OFFICIAL MAP. This will get you close. The rest is up to you.
Please read: this is a research aid, not a ruling. The Park Overlap Finder shows places where our maps suggest the boundaries of two or more POTA parks may overlap. It is a starting point for your own research only. The Kentucky Portable Club cannot confirm or deny that any location listed here is a valid multi-park activation site. Park boundaries change, maps can be out of date or imprecise, and private land, closures and local rules may apply even inside a boundary. Before you count an activation, check each park's official boundary map from the managing agency, make sure you are on public land that is open to the public, and follow POTA's activation rules. POTA sets the rules, and activations run on the honor system: it's up to each activator to follow them. You are responsible for confirming your own location. KPC is an independent ham radio club, not part of Parks on the Air®.
The pins get you to the area of a possible 2-fer or 3-fer; they are not confirmed activation spots. Put together by the Kentucky Portable Club from public map data and not field-checked at every spot. Once there, make sure you and your gear are on public land inside every park (within 100 ft of the trail or the water for trail and river parks; national forest spots count only on Forest Service-owned land). Riverbank pins have not been checked against the actual water line. 2-fer means two POTA parks only.
