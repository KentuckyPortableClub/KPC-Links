Kentucky POTA Multi-Park Spots

Places in Kentucky where you may be able to work two or three POTA parks at once, with GPS points you can drive to, checked against the POTA rules: shared boundaries on land that belongs to both parks, the Sheltowee Trace and Pine Mountain Trail through other parks, and Trail of Tears and Lewis & Clark sites and Ohio River banks.

Planning tool only. Research and confirm every spot before you go. Put together by the Kentucky Portable Club from public map data; not field-checked at every spot or approved by POTA. You and your gear must be on public land inside every park (within 100 ft of the trail or river for trail and river parks), and national forest spots count only on Forest Service-owned land. Check the official maps and the POTA Rules. 2-fer means two POTA parks only.

Compiled by the Kentucky Portable Club, Oct 13, 2026, from the KPC site data, the Forest Service land ownership map and USGS river outlines: official state and federal park boundaries, the KPC park guide and its parking and entrance points, the Sheltowee Trace and Pine Mountain Trail lines, and the National Park Service's Trail of Tears and Lewis & Clark route lines. "Trail enters" points are where the mapped trail crosses the park line, often a road or trailhead. The Daniel Boone National Forest outline includes private land, and much of the Trail of Tears follows today's roads, so confirm you're on public park land. The Lewis & Clark trail in Kentucky is the Ohio River itself; do your own research before counting riverbank spots.
