Kentucky POTA Multi-Park Spots

Places in Kentucky where you may be able to work two or three POTA parks at once, with GPS guide pins that get you to the area, checked against the POTA rules: overlapping boundaries on land that belongs to both parks, the Sheltowee Trace and Pine Mountain Trail through other parks, Trail of Tears and Lewis & Clark sites, Ohio River banks, and Trail of Tears Water Route shores on Kentucky Lake and the Mississippi.

GPS pins are guides only. They get you to the area of a possible 2-fer or 3-fer; they are not confirmed activation spots. Once there, check that you are inside every park and, for trail and river parks, within 100 ft of the trail or the water. Riverbank pins have not been checked against the actual water line. Park names and numbers match the POTA Kentucky park list.
Planning tool only. Research and confirm every spot before you go. Put together by the Kentucky Portable Club from public map data; not field-checked at every spot or approved by POTA. You and your gear must be on public land inside every park (within 100 ft of the trail or river for trail and river parks), and national forest spots count only on Forest Service-owned land. Check the official maps and the POTA Rules. 2-fer means two POTA parks only.

Compiled by the Kentucky Portable Club, Oct 13, 2026 (pins rechecked the same day: names matched to the POTA park list, setup pins moved at least 100 ft inside the boundary lines, riverbank pins marked approximate), from the KPC site data, the Forest Service land ownership map and USGS river outlines: official state and federal park boundaries, the KPC park guide and its parking and entrance points, the Sheltowee Trace and Pine Mountain Trail lines, and the National Park Service's Trail of Tears and Lewis & Clark route lines. "Trail enters" points are where the mapped trail crosses the park line, often a road or trailhead. The Daniel Boone National Forest outline includes private land, and much of the Trail of Tears follows today's roads, so confirm you're on public park land. The Lewis & Clark trail in Kentucky is the Ohio River itself; do your own research before counting riverbank spots. Wickliffe Mounds, Big Bone Lick and Golden Pond Visitor Center are not within 100 ft of the river or the trail; they are listed because they are on the National Park Service's Lewis & Clark "Visitor Centers in Kentucky" list. Trail of Tears spots were checked against the NPS Trail of Tears route map.
